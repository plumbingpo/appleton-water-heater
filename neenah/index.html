Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Neenah

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
