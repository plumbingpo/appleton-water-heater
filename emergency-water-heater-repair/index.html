How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Blocking the discharge pipe, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are mistakes we see far too often.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution instead of relying on a sniff test, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
